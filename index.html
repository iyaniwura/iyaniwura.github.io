About

I am an Associate Research Scientist in Hyrien Lab, and also a member of the Vaccine & Immunology Statistical Center (VISC), in the Biostatistics, Bioinformatics and Epidemiology (BBE) Program, of the Vaccine and Infectious Disease Division (VIDD) at Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center. I am an MGB-SIAM Early Career Fellow.

Prior to this, I was a Post-doctoral Research Associate at the Theoretical Biology and Biophysics group of the Los Alamos National Laboratory, New Mexico, USA, working with Alan S. Perelson, Ruy M. Ribeiro, and Ruian Ke. I obtained my Ph.D. from the Department of Mathematics and the Institute of Applied Mathematics (IAM) of the University of British Columbia (UBC), Vancouver, working with Michael Ward, Colin Macdonald, and Daniel Coombs.

Before my studies at UBC, I obtained a Masters degree in Mathematical Sciences from Stellenbosch University, South Africa, through the fully funded scholarship program offered by the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS), Cape Town, South Africa.

Research

I am interested in developing mathematical modeling and analytical frameworks for analyzing biological systems. My current research at Fred Hutch focuses on developing data-driven mathematical modelling frameworks for understanding B-cell dynamics during vaccine-induced immune response, and using the framework to optimize gremline-targeting and sequential immunization strategies in the context of HIV vaccine. The techniques developed in this research will be applied to design vaccine regimens and improve clinical trial outcome, thereby reducing the time and cost for developing and effective vaccine for HIV.

I am also interested in other aspects of infectious disease modeling both within a host and at the population level, and modeling immune response to infections. Furthermore, I am interested in developing and analyzing reaction-diffusion systems, and developing theoretical framework for analyzing first passage time theory for passive and active Brownian systems.

I use ordinary differential equations (ODEs), partial differential equations (PDEs), agent-based models, and statistical and machine-learning models. To analyze the ODE and PDE models, I employ analytical techniques such as dynamical systems theory, perturbation theory, asymptotic analysis, and biophysical methods, and solve them numerically using finite difference, finite element, and closest point methods.
